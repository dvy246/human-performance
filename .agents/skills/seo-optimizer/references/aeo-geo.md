# AI Answer Engine Optimization (AEO/GEO)

## What this is and why it's now a core part of SEO, not an add-on

Search behavior in 2026 increasingly routes through systems that generate a direct answer rather than a list of blue links — Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and similar. These systems extract and cite specific, self-contained passages rather than "ranking" a page the traditional way. Content structured for this gets cited; content that isn't, doesn't — regardless of how good its traditional on-page SEO is. This is a structural writing discipline, not a separate technical system, and it costs nothing extra to apply once you know the pattern.

## The core pattern: direct-answer-first

Under every header that poses or implies a question, the first 1-2 sentences must directly and completely answer it, in plain, self-contained language that doesn't depend on surrounding context to make sense. Supporting detail, nuance, and caveats come after, not before.

**Weak (buries the answer):**
> ## How long does it take to improve reaction time?
> Reaction time is influenced by many factors including age, sleep, practice frequency, and the specific test methodology used. Improvement timelines vary considerably based on these factors and individual variation is significant, with some studies suggesting...

**Strong (direct-answer-first):**
> ## How long does it take to improve reaction time?
> Most people see a measurable improvement within 2-3 weeks of consistent daily practice (10-15 minutes/day). The rate of improvement then slows — early gains come from familiarity with the test format itself, while gains after week 3 reflect genuine reaction-speed improvement. [supporting detail follows]

## Headers as real questions

Phrase H2/H3 headers as the actual question a person (or an AI system summarizing for a person) would ask — not an abstract label. "What affects your measured score" beats "Contributing Factors." This also directly supports the on-page header guidance in `references/on-page-seo.md` — it's the same discipline applied with citation-extraction in mind.

## Self-contained, extractable statements

A sentence that depends heavily on unstated context ("this makes it faster than the alternative") is hard for an extraction system to cite safely. A sentence that names its subject and claim explicitly ("CogniArena's Flick Trainer measures target-acquisition speed in milliseconds, unlike click-counting tools that only measure raw click frequency") is safely extractable on its own. Write key claims — especially in FAQ answers and direct-answer-first openers — so they'd make sense pulled completely out of the page and shown alone.

## Checking competitive citation position

If named competitors exist for this content's topic, check (by actually searching, not assuming) whether they're currently being cited or summarized by AI answer engines for the relevant queries. If they are and this content isn't structured to compete for that same treatment, that's a specific, actionable gap — note exactly which structural pattern (direct-answer-first, self-contained statements, question-phrased headers) is present on their page and missing here.
