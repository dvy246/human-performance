# Content Quality & E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust)

## Why this phase matters independently of keywords/schema

A page can be technically perfect and keyword-correct and still fail to rank or convert if it reads as generic, unsourced, or untrustworthy. Search engines' quality systems and human readers both penalize this — it's not a separate concern from "real" SEO, it's a core part of it.

## Sourcing & Citations

- Every factual, statistical, or benchmark-style claim cites a specific, real, findable source — a named study, author, year, or organization, ideally with a link. "Studies show..." with nothing named behind it does not clear this bar.
- If a claim can't be properly sourced, it should be either removed, or clearly relabeled as an estimate/opinion rather than presented as established fact.
- Never invent a citation or attribute a false provenance to a number (e.g., claiming "based on published research" for a number that's actually just an internal estimate) — this is a materially worse trust failure than the same number presented honestly as an estimate.

## Authorship & Expertise Signals

- Any page where the reader's trust depends on expertise (how-to guides, methodology explanations, health/finance/safety-adjacent content, benchmark/comparison data) should carry a real byline with a genuine, specific credential — not a placeholder name or a generic "Our Team" attribution.
- An "About" or "Our Team" page should say who is actually behind the site, with real, checkable specifics — not just a mission-statement paragraph with no names.

## Avoiding AI-Slop Patterns

Check content specifically for these patterns and rewrite where found — they're both a reader-trust problem and, increasingly, a ranking risk as search engines get better at detecting generic AI-generated filler:
- Vague filler transitions and hedging ("It's important to note that...", "In today's fast-paced world...").
- Repetitive sentence structures/openers across paragraphs.
- Generic claims with no specific, concrete detail behind them ("many people find this helpful" instead of a real number, example, or mechanism).
- Content that reads as templated across multiple pages with only nouns swapped — each page should read like it was actually written about its specific subject, not generated from a shared skeleton.

## Internal Linking

- Every page links to at least 1-2 other genuinely relevant pages.
- Every page is linked from at least one other page (no orphans).
- Hub/category pages link down to every relevant child page; child pages link back up and sideways to genuinely related siblings — not a forced link just to hit a quota.

## Preserving Existing Trust Content

If a page already has a disclaimer, safety notice, or legal notice, any SEO-motivated edit must preserve its meaning and visibility — don't trim it for "cleaner" copy, and don't move it somewhere less visible to make room for keyword-optimized text.

## Evidence to produce for this phase

A list of every unsourced claim found and how it was resolved (sourced, softened, or removed); confirmation of author/byline presence where it matters; and a specific note on any AI-slop pattern found and rewritten, with a before/after example.
