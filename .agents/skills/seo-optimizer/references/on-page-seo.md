# On-Page Optimization

Apply consistently across every page or page template. This phase assumes Phase 2 (keyword mapping) is already done — every page should already have a defined primary/secondary keyword set before you optimize its on-page elements around them.

## Title Tags

- Primary keyword near the front, phrased the way a US searcher would recognize it.
- Under ~60 characters (so it doesn't truncate in search results).
- Written to earn the click, not just restate the keyword — a title tag is an ad for the page, not a label.
- **Unique across the entire site** — scan programmatically for duplicates, don't spot-check a handful of pages.

## Meta Descriptions

- Genuinely descriptive of what the reader gets on that page.
- Includes the primary keyword naturally (not forced).
- Under ~155 characters.
- Unique per page — same duplicate-check discipline as titles.

## Headers

- **Exactly one H1 per page**, matching the page's primary keyword intent — not necessarily identical text to the title tag.
- H2/H3 hierarchy reflects real content structure — no skipped levels (H1 straight to H3), no purely decorative headers with no organizing purpose.
- Where it fits naturally, secondary keywords appear in H2/H3 text — never forced in if it breaks natural phrasing.
- Headers phrased as real questions where the content answers a question (this also feeds directly into `references/aeo-geo.md`'s citation patterns).

## Body Content

- Depth determined by what's needed to fully answer the query — not a fixed word-count target. A complete 400-word answer beats a padded 1200-word one.
- No keyword stuffing: no exact-match phrase repeating more than roughly once per 150 words. Rewrite denser sections in natural language.
- Genuinely useful content that would satisfy the reader without needing to click back to search results and try another page — this is literally the bar search engines themselves use to judge "helpful" content.

## URLs

- Short, keyword-relevant slugs.
- No unnecessary parameters on canonical/indexable URLs.
- Consistent formatting sitewide (hyphens not underscores, consistent casing, consistent trailing-slash convention).

## Images

- Descriptive alt text that actually describes the image (not a keyword dump).
- Appropriately compressed and sized for the display context.
- Lazy-loaded when below the fold.

## Evidence to produce for this phase

For a sample of representative pages (or every page if the count is small), show the actual before/after of title, meta description, and H1 — not just a statement that they were "optimized." For a full-site pass, also show the results of the sitewide duplicate-title and duplicate-meta-description scan.
