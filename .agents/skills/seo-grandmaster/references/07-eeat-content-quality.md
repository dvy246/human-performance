# E-E-A-T & Content Quality

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust are the difference between content that ranks and
holds its ranking, versus content that spikes and fades. This matters for every site, and it's a hard
gate — not just a nice-to-have — for anything touching YMYL territory (health, finance, legal, safety).

## Sourced, specific claims

Every factual or statistical claim needs a real, findable source — never "studies show" with nothing
named, and never an invented citation attached to what's actually just an internal estimate. If a claim
can't be properly sourced, label it honestly as an estimate/opinion rather than dressing it up as fact.

## Real authorship and credentials

Generic "Our Team" attribution with no real names undercuts trust anywhere expertise is the actual
selling point (how-to content, methodology explanations, comparisons, benchmarks). Where a page's value
depends on the author's expertise, use real names and genuine, specific credentials — and make sure the
site's About page explains, honestly and specifically, who runs it and why it's credible, rather than a
vague mission statement.

## Avoid AI-slop content patterns (checkable, not just a vibe)

Read every page specifically checking for:
- Vague hedging filler phrases that say nothing concrete.
- Repetitive paragraph-opener patterns across sections.
- Generic claims that could apply to any competitor's page on the same topic with the name swapped.
- Templated pages in a programmatic set that differ only by a swapped noun with no other real depth.

If AI-assisted drafting was used, this is exactly what a real editorial pass needs to catch and fix —
publishing without that pass is the actual risk, not AI assistance itself.

## Build topic clusters, not isolated pages

One strong pillar/hub page plus genuinely supporting, interlinked articles around it tends to
out-perform scattered one-off pages targeting the same broad topic individually — both because it
signals topical completeness to search engines, and because it gives users (and internal link equity) a
real path to follow. When auditing a site's content:
- Identify the realistic full scope of the site's core subject — what would a genuinely comprehensive
  resource cover here that's currently missing?
- Check that hub pages link down to every real child page, and child pages link back up and sideways to
  genuinely related siblings (not a forced link just to hit a linking quota).
- Treat isolated, unlinked pages on a core topic as a structural gap to close, not just a linking nice-
  to-have.

## Internal linking specifics

- Descriptive anchor text (not "click here" or "read more").
- No orphan pages: every indexable page should have at least one real inbound internal link from a
  genuinely related page — sitemap presence alone doesn't substitute for this.
- No broken internal or external links — check systematically, not by spot-checking; external sources
  cited for E-E-A-T purposes go stale over time and need periodic re-verification.

## Preserve existing trust content

Never trim or bury an existing disclaimer, safety notice, or legal notice to make room for SEO-optimized
copy. If a page needs to be shorter, find that room elsewhere — trust content is never the thing to cut.

## Evidence to produce for this phase

For each page audited: the specific claims checked for sourcing (with what was found), the authorship
information present or missing, any AI-slop patterns found and how they were rewritten, and the topic-
cluster/internal-linking map showing hub-to-child and child-to-sibling connections.
