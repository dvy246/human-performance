# Google Search Essentials & Spam Policy Compliance

Google actively ranks sites lower, or removes them from results entirely, for violating Search
Essentials. Check every site/page against all five categories below — a violation in any one of them
caps or eliminates ranking potential regardless of how well everything else in this skill is executed.

## The five categories

**1. Thin Content**
Sites with little to no original content, auto-generated text, or scraped information that fails to
provide genuinely helpful value. Check: does every page have real, substantive, non-templated value —
not padding, not a swapped-noun copy of another page on the same site, not content that could have been
produced without any real research or expertise behind it?

**2. Keyword Stuffing**
Unnaturally packing pages with keywords to manipulate rankings instead of writing naturally. Concrete,
checkable rule (see `05-keyword-onpage-strategy.md` for the full mechanics): no exact-match
phrase should repeat more than roughly once per 150 words. Anything denser gets rewritten in natural
language — this is treated as a real violation risk, not a minor style note.

**3. Bad Link Practices**
Buying backlinks, using private blog networks (PBNs), or linking to spammy "neighborhoods." This skill
never recommends or facilitates any of these. If a site's existing backlink profile is suspected to
include manipulative links, the correct action is disavowal through Search Console, not something this
skill can execute directly — name that as the tool and next step.

**Earning real backlinks (the legitimate counterpart, worth naming explicitly)**: backlinks remain a
heavily-weighted signal, and the only durable way to earn them is by producing something genuinely
worth linking to — original data, a genuinely useful tool, comprehensive coverage nothing else offers —
and then making sure the right people (journalists, forum communities, other site owners in the niche)
actually know it exists. That second half (outreach/promotion of genuinely good content) is legitimate
and different from buying/farming links; the difference is whether the link is earned because the
content deserves it or manufactured to look earned.

**4. Poor User Experience (UX)**
Intrusive interstitial pop-ups, extremely slow loading speeds, or lack of mobile-friendliness. See
`02-technical-seo-audit.md` for the full checklist (Core Web Vitals, mobile rendering, interstitials).

**5. Lack of Trust (E-E-A-T)**
Sites lacking Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness — especially damaging for
"Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL) topics like health, finance, legal, and safety. See
`07-eeat-content-quality.md` for the full checklist. **YMYL topics require materially higher
scrutiny**: real, checkable author credentials, real sourcing, and honest framing of any advisory
content — never treat a YMYL page with the same trust bar as a purely informational/utility page.

## If a site isn't performing well

Before assuming a Search Essentials violation, also consider (and say so honestly rather than
defaulting to "you must be violating a policy"): genuinely high competition in the niche, or a real
but fixable technical/on-page gap covered elsewhere in this skill. Two concrete next steps worth naming
to the user, since this skill can't execute them directly:
- **Google Search Help Community** — for troubleshooting specific ranking/indexing problems with
  community and Googler input.
- **Google Search Console** — the authoritative source for whether a manual action or algorithmic
  demotion has actually been applied to the site; don't guess at this when Search Console can confirm
  it directly.

## Honesty rule specific to this file

Never tell a user their site "will definitely rank" or state a specific probability of avoiding a
penalty. The five categories above are real, documented risk factors — checking and fixing them
genuinely improves the odds, but "genuinely improves the odds" and "guarantees an outcome" are different
claims, and only the first one is honest to make.
